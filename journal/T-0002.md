## What this is

The half of the codex that could not be harvested. Nine chapters of design
authority that existed only in the founder's head, written from a 40-question
interview answered in one pass.

33 of 40 answered. Six chapters reach `status: authoritative`; four sit at
`draft`, each blocked on one named question rather than on general
incompleteness.

## The rule I applied for authoritative vs draft

A chapter is authoritative only if **every** spec that would anchor to it can be
written. Not "mostly complete" — completeness measured against what specs need.

That is stricter than it sounds and it is why 05, 08, 09 and 11 stayed draft
despite each being 80% answered. Chapter 08 has settled answers on who builds
the schedule, publication timing, breaks, swaps, overtime and the custody chain
— but 8.3, the exception taxonomy, is what *enters* the custody chain, so
`SPEC-SCH-004` cannot be written. Eighty percent of a chapter is not eighty
percent of a spec; it is a spec with a hole in the middle.

The alternative — mark it authoritative and let the spec bench the gap later —
is precisely the unspecced-layer-under-a-specced-one failure D-010 exists to
prevent, arriving one layer lower down.

## The contradiction

The single most valuable thing this interview produced was a conflict, and it
would have been very easy to miss.

**3.1** says throughput only at employee grain — food cost, labor cost and sales
totals are *invalid* for an individual because they control none of them.
**5.2** puts crew on a weighted scorecard of "sales / food / labor / training".

Same sitting, both answered confidently, mutually exclusive. If I had written 03
and 05 in isolation — or written 05 first and treated 03 as a refinement — the
system would have shipped an employee bonus paying on metrics the measurement
chapter calls meaningless, and nobody would have found it until a crew member
asked why their bonus moved when they were not working.

Benched as **B-0009** with a recommended resolution (team-based: the crew member
shares in their *store's* sales/food/labor result, which keeps those metrics at
store grain where 03 permits them, plus their own throughput and training). Not
resolved — D-016's guard is explicit that where two sources disagree, that is a
bench item and never a silent amendment to make them agree.

This is the argument for interviewing a whole domain at once rather than
chapter-by-chapter as each phase arrives. Piecemeal, these two answers are
months apart and the contradiction is invisible.

## What I refused to fill

Seven residues, all benched rather than inferred:

- **B-0011** — 8.3 unanswered. The exception taxonomy.
- **B-0012** — three money parameters: deposit ageing (9.4), royalty basis and
  tolerance (9.6), material invoice price variance (9.7).
- **B-0013** — 11.1 unanswered. The real-time vs cadence routing table.
- **B-0010** — 7.1's "everything you said" is genuinely ambiguous about I-9 and
  W-4, because the list I offered had already flagged them as probably
  payroll's.
- **B-0007** — 4.5 was a stated default, not answered. Silence on a stated
  default is weak consent.
- **B-0008** — a new fork 3.1 opened rather than closed.
- **B-0009** — the contradiction above.

B-0010 deserves a note. It would have been easy to read "everything you said" as
covering it and move on. But an I-9 carries its own federal retention rule —
three years after hire or one year after termination, whichever is later, *not*
the seven years answered in 7.2 — and is conventionally stored separately from
the personnel file so that an I-9 audit does not expose everything else.
Modelling it as one more credential row gets both the retention and the
isolation wrong. A wrong guess here is a compliance defect, not a schema
inconvenience.

B-0007 is the one I felt most tempted to let slide. The metric list is additive
and low-risk — a target column added later is a nullable column and a backfill.
But "I stated a default and they did not object" is not a decision, and Rule 1
does not have a low-stakes exemption. It is recorded in chapter 04 as *assumed*,
in those words.

## Two answers whose consequences I recorded rather than argued with

**8.6B — overtime is detection, not prediction.** Option A was predictive
warning; B was warn-on-exceed. B was chosen. That removes the mid-week "who is
about to cross 40 while you can still move a shift" mechanic I had pitched as
high-value in the plan.

Written into chapter 08 as a plain statement of what the system does and does
not do, with the cost named and a note that the reversal is one line of
arithmetic because both scheduled and worked hours are already present. Not
re-argued. The decision was made from a clearly worded pair of options and
re-litigating it in the codex would be exactly the "reasonable-but-wrong drift"
Burst exists to kill — just in the opposite direction.

**10.5 — all three reward types.** Recognition post *and* points *and* gift
card. That is the expensive branch of the fork I flagged: points that accumulate
require a ledger with balances, not a list of winners. So
`ops_recognition_points` is append-only with redemptions as negative rows, and a
balance is a sum, never a stored mutable integer. A gift card is a payout and
runs the same review chain as a bonus.

## Smaller things that came out of writing it down

**6.2A forced a two-table people model.** "One person, many store assignments"
sounds like a one-line answer. But `stg_timeclock` keys on
`(clerk_id, restaurant_number)`, so an employee working two stores is currently
two unrelated clerks. One table cannot represent this: it needs
`ref_employees` plus `ref_employee_assignments`, with the assignment carrying
the `clerk_id`. Everything downstream then keys on the person — which is also
what makes 8.6's overtime threshold correct, since hours must sum across stores
and no single store's view would ever show someone at 50 hours.

**Role is a property of the assignment, not the person.** Same reasoning:
someone can be shift lead at one store and crew at another. Where a single role
is needed — bonus eligibility — the highest active role wins.

**The stub-roster answer needed a third state.** 6.1A auto-creates a row on
first clock-in, which means rows exist with no wage. A missing wage is not a
zero wage, so an incomplete employee is *excluded* from labor-dollar
calculations and the schedule guard reports "partially costed" rather than
passing. A labor cost that silently omits an employee is worse than one that
admits it is incomplete.

**11.4 has a consequence nobody states.** Confirming that certificates never go
to Discord means expiry nudges can say *that* something expires but never what
it contains. "Your food handler card expires in 30 days — open your page" is the
entire permissible message. Generalised in chapter 11 as: Discord carries
notification, the app carries content.
