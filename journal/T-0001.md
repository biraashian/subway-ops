## What this is

The harvest half of Part 2. D-017 blocks new specs until a design authority
exists above them; this lands the part of that authority that can be *derived*
from documents already in the repo. The other half — chapters 03 through 11 —
cannot be derived from anything, because the domain they describe has never been
written down. That half is a founder interview and is deliberately not attempted
here.

## What was harvested, and from where

Four chapters, all sourced from the pre-Burst `CLAUDE.md` (git history at
`4b499e2`), `SUBWAY_OPS_BUILD_PLAYBOOK.md` §1/§4/§5/§6, and the dated decision
log at the bottom of `DECISIONS.md`.

- **00-INSTITUTION** — the thesis (reconcile / remember / recognize, plus the
  review chain), the 15 stores, the four territories, the role ladder, and the
  scope boundaries.
- **01-CALENDAR** — the 02:00 business day, the Wed–Tue week, the single

## Two things I changed rather than copied

**The role ladder is new material, not harvest.** The old documents named an
owner and three field managers and stopped there. Chapter 00 states five roles
with explicit scopes and two rules that follow from the table: a person sees
their own scope and nothing above it, enforced server-side; and approval never
skips a link. Both are implied by D-019 and D-022 rather than stated anywhere,
and leaving them implicit is how a crew page ends up leaking a payroll figure.
Flagging it here because "harvested" should not be allowed to smuggle in
invention unremarked.

**Territory membership is time-varying.** The playbook treats a field manager's
cluster as a static fact from a CSV. It is not — stores move between managers.
Anything reporting by territory has to resolve membership as of the period being
reported, or last quarter's numbers silently rewrite themselves when a store
changes hands. The existing `mart_field_manager_portfolio` almost certainly does
not do this. I did not chase it: it is a retro-spec finding and belongs in a
bench item raised by `/spec`, not in a codex_update.

## The gate had to ship in the same change

`scripts/verify.sh` FAILS if `codex/` exists without an executable
`verify-codex.sh` — I wrote that guard in T-0000 because the upstream kit
skipped silently there and its own errata calls it a vacuous-pass hole. So
creating the codex without its gate would have reddened the build immediately.
That is the design working: the guard fired at exactly the moment it was
supposed to, on its author.

`codex/verify-codex.sh` runs four checks: internal link integrity (ported from
the cleanup kit essentially verbatim), chapter-index consistency, unique chapter
prefixes, and no placeholder markers in a chapter marked
`status: authoritative`.

That last one is the one I care about. `authoritative` means specs may anchor to
a chapter; a chapter carrying a TODO is not finished, and a spec anchored to a
hole inherits the hole. That is precisely the unspecced-layer-under-a-specced-one
failure D-010 exists to prevent, which is why the status field is machine-checked
rather than honour-system.

The index check is bidirectional on purpose: a directory missing from the index
is unread, and an index row pointing at a missing directory is how "we already
decided that" turns out to be a link to nothing. Chapters still marked
"not written" are expected to have no directory — that is the honest state, and
the check knows it.

## Calibration

Same discipline as T-0000: all four checks proven against a deliberately broken
copy of the codex — a dangling link, an unindexed chapter directory, a duplicate
`02-` prefix, and a TODO added to an authoritative chapter. Four for four.

Cheaper than T-0000's calibration and it found nothing wrong, which is a
slightly disappointing result and the right one to record honestly: the harness
worked first time here because these checks are much simpler than
`check_track_job`, which is the one that had real logic to get wrong.

## Legacy

`SUBWAY_OPS_BUILD_PLAYBOOK.md` and `SUBWAY_OPS_TASKS.md` moved to `legacy/` with
a README stating they are no longer authoritative and that the codex wins any
disagreement.

The README carries a specific caution about the task tracker: it marks every
task `[~]` complete, including a 12-month backfill that had not been run when it
was written and an audit whose findings were three months stale by the time
anyone re-read them. It records what was intended, not what was true. Citing it
as evidence that something works is exactly the mistake that made the last
planning pass open on a false premise.

The pre-Burst decision log stays at the bottom of `DECISIONS.md` rather than
moving, because `/sweep` diffs live state against that file and splitting it
would hide half the history from the check.

`README.md`'s two links to the retired documents were dead the moment they
moved; rewritten to point at the codex, the law, the board, and the bench, and
to state the one command that decides whether work is done.

## What is deliberately absent

Chapters 03–11 have no directories and are marked "not written" in the index.
There is no placeholder scaffolding for them, on purpose — an empty chapter with
a heading structure invites someone to fill it in with something plausible, and
plausible is the failure mode Rule 1 exists to stop. The interview comes first.
