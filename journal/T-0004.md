# T-0004 — People schema: persons, assignments, wages, identity log

Date: 2026-08-13
Branch: `forge/T-0004`
Type: schema_change
Anchors: SPEC-PPL-001 · REQ-002, REQ-003, REQ-005, REQ-006 · AC-011, AC-014, AC-022, AC-031
Decisions: none new; reads D-020, D-032, and B-0014/B-0015's answers

## What was built

Migration `021_people.sql` and `tests/test_people_schema.py` (21 tests). Four
new tables and one altered constraint:

- `ref_employees` — the person.
- `ref_employee_assignments` — one person's presence at one store.
- `ref_employee_wages` — effective-dated pay, never updated in place.
- `ops_employee_identity_log` — append-only audit of merge and split.
- `ref_users.role` — the CHECK widened from three values to six.

After this migration the system can represent a human being. It has known about
stores since migration 001 and about clerk PINs since 008; it has never known
about people, and scheduling, timecards, training, certificates, KPIs, bonuses
and recognition all key on one.

No behavior ships here. T-0004 is schema, so every test asserts something the
*database* guarantees rather than something Python does: constraints that reject
bad rows, keys that refuse duplicates, and the exact column set SPEC §5 states.

## The absence that took the most thought

`ref_employees` has no natural-key UNIQUE constraint, and writing a table with
no way to say "this person already exists" felt wrong until I went back to the
measurement behind B-0014.

250 distinct `clerk_id`s across 399 `(clerk_id, store)` pairs. **56 clerk_ids
carry more than one name.** PIN `8` appears under five different names at five
stores; PIN `1998` under four names at nine stores. Names are no better —
`samir, samir` appears at five stores under six clerk_ids.

There is no field here, and no combination of fields, that identifies a human.
Any UNIQUE I invented would do one of two things: reject legitimate rows, or —
much worse — silently fuse two people's hours, wages and certificates. B-0014
settled that under-linking is repairable by one office action and over-linking
is not repairable at all, because by the time anyone notices, the merged history
has been paid out.

So the constraint that would normally live in the schema lives in code (AC-005's
linking rule) and its repairs are audited in `ops_employee_identity_log`. I
wrote the reasoning into the migration at the point of absence, because "why
does this table have no unique key" is exactly the question a future reader will
ask, and the answer is measured rather than stylistic.

## Two role vocabularies, and a comment on `main` that is wrong about them

This was the one place I had to read carefully rather than implement directly.

T-0004's task file says, in a test-name comment, `# crew, shift_lead added`.
SPEC-PPL-001 §5 says the CHECK "expands to add `store_manager`, `office`,
`crew`" — three additions, no `shift_lead`. Those cannot both be right.

Chasing it produced a fuller picture than I expected:

- **codex 00**'s ladder table names five roles — owner, field_manager, office,
  store_manager, crew — and describes `readonly` separately as a mentor or
  lender view that sees no personally identifying data. No `shift_lead`.
- **codex 06 "Roles"** names six — crew, shift_lead, store_manager,
  field_manager, office, owner — and says role is a property of the
  **assignment**, not of the person, because someone can be a shift lead at one
  store and crew at another.
- **SPEC REQ-006** orders exactly codex 06's six for `effective_role`.
- **Migration 020** (T-0003, merged) constrains `ops_worklist_items.assigned_role`
  to codex 06's six, while its comment cites *codex 00* and asserts the set is
  "duplicated with `ref_users.role`, which T-0004 widens to the same six".

So the two vocabularies genuinely differ, and they differ for a reason:

| | contents |
|---|---|
| `ops_worklist_items.assigned_role` | owner · field_manager · office · store_manager · **shift_lead** · crew |
| `ref_users.role` after 021 | owner · field_manager · office · store_manager · crew · **readonly** |

The first is a **job** ladder — what someone does on a shift, and therefore who
a worklist item can be assigned to. The second is an **access** vocabulary for
the admin surface: `readonly` is nobody's job, and `shift_lead` carries no admin
access distinct from crew's. Six each, differing by one element in each
direction.

I implemented SPEC §5 — three additions, no `shift_lead` — on the read-upward
rule: the spec outranks the task file, and the task file's comment is the lower
layer. That is precedence rather than a judgment call, which is why it did not
become a bench item.

**What I could not fix.** Migration 020's comment is wrong on both counts: it
cites codex 00 for a set that comes from codex 06, and it promises a duplication
that 021 does not create. 020 merged this afternoon and is frozen absolutely
under D-032 — no exception, no size threshold, and the fact that I wrote the
comment myself four hours ago changes nothing. So the correction lives in 021,
at the site of the `ALTER`, where a reader tracing the role vocabulary will
actually be looking. `tests/test_worklist.py` also carries a docstring claiming
the CHECK "must match codex/00-INSTITUTION's ladder exactly"; that file is not
in this task's DELIVERABLES and I left it alone.

`test_ref_users_role_still_rejects_an_unknown_role` pins `shift_lead` as
deliberately absent rather than accidentally missing. If someone later merges
the two vocabularies, that test fails and forces the conversation instead of
letting it happen quietly.

## Where I did less than I wanted to

Two places, both the same lesson from T-0003's audit.

**`ref_employee_assignments.role` has no CHECK.** SPEC §5 specifies exactly
`role: TEXT NOT NULL DEFAULT 'crew'` and nothing more. T-0003 constrained its
role column and argued well for it — a typo'd role assigns work to nobody and
the item stays invisible forever — and that argument applies here too. I did not
add the constraint. B-0026 found T-0003 RED for shipping structure past the
stated shape, and "but my extra structure is good" was exactly the defense that
did not survive. If this column should be constrained, that is a one-line spec
amendment and a later migration.

**`ops_employee_identity_log` has no per-action CHECK.** A merge names `from`
and `to`; a split names an `assignment_id` and the person it moved to. A
constraint enforcing "merge requires from, split requires assignment_id" is
obviously correct and obviously unlicensed, so the three FK columns are simply
nullable. `test_identity_log_records_a_merge_and_a_split` inserts one of each so
the nullability reads as intentional rather than as laxness.

The one place I did add something SPEC §5 omits is `log_id BIGSERIAL PRIMARY
KEY`. A log table with no primary key cannot address its own rows, every `ops_*`
table in this schema has one, and `ops_worklist_items` set the precedent last
week. I named the exception explicitly in the migration and in the shape test's
docstring rather than letting it ride, because the whole point of the shape test
is that extras are visible.

## The shape test asserts equality, not containment

`test_table_columns_match_spec_section_5` compares the full column set of each
table against SPEC §5's prose with `==`. A `>=` would have passed T-0003's
`resolved_by` happily, which is precisely how that column reached `main`. Set
equality catches gold-plating and gaps with the same assertion, and it fails
loudly the moment someone adds a column without touching the spec.

Writing it made me notice how much of §5 is prose rather than a table. Four
tables' worth of columns transcribed by hand into a parametrised test is exactly
the kind of transcription that goes stale silently — but it goes stale *loudly*
here, because the test compares against the database rather than against another
copy of the prose.

## What the drift gate could not show me

The declaration lists four new tables. It does not mention the `ALTER` on
`ref_users_role_check`, because `schema_snapshot.py` captures tables and columns
and nothing else — no CHECK, no UNIQUE, no foreign key, no index predicate, no
NOT NULL.

That means the single most security-adjacent line in this migration — widening
who may hold an account on the admin surface — produced **zero** drift lines. I
verified it rather than assuming: four changes reported, all of them new tables,
gate green.

Raised as **B-0029**. What makes it worth a bench item rather than a shrug is
the history: B-0022 chose the acknowledgement-file mechanism over migration-
tracing specifically because tracing "cannot see an ALTER that a migration makes
by accident, which is precisely the case worth catching". The mechanism we
adopted to preserve that property does not have it either — the declaration is
sound, but what it is declared *over* is narrower than the review it supports.
T-0003's `dismissed` fix widened an index predicate and produced no drift line
either; nobody noticed at the time, including the audit.

I put the constraint change in the declaration's prose so this task's diff is
honest, but prose is not a check.

## The process slip, which is a repeat

I ran this task on `main` and branched only when it was time to commit — the
same thing T-0003's journal flagged, in the same words, three tasks ago. Nothing
was harmed: nothing was committed to `main`, `git checkout -b` carried the
working tree over intact, and the gate ran on `forge/T-0004`.

Recording it as a repeat rather than as a fresh observation, because a habit
that has now recurred after being written down is a different fact from a
one-off. The failure mode it protects against — two tasks in flight, one
accidentally built on the other's uncommitted state — has not bitten yet only
because the queue has been strictly serial.

## Verification, per AC

Each verified individually against the database, not inferred from a green
suite:

- **AC-011** (insert, never modify) — `test_wage_pk_rejects_duplicate_effective_date`
  proves the composite PK refuses a second rate for the same person on the same
  date; `test_wage_history_accumulates_across_dates` proves a raise is a new row
  and the old rate survives it.
- **AC-014** (reject non-positive) — `test_hourly_rate_must_be_positive`
  parametrised over `0`, `-0.0001` and `-15.5000`, all rejected by the CHECK;
  `test_hourly_rate_accepts_a_positive_rate` proves the constraint does not
  reject valid pay.
- **AC-022** (termination sets date and flag) —
  `test_termination_sets_date_and_flag_without_deleting`, which also asserts the
  row still exists afterwards (INV-009).
- **AC-031** (nullable unique identifiers, no auth) —
  `test_phone_is_unique_and_nullable` and
  `test_discord_user_id_is_unique_and_nullable`. Both check the *nullable* half
  too: two people with no phone must not collide with each other, which is the
  operationally important case given that almost none of the 250 have either
  identifier yet. Nothing in this task authenticates anybody (NG-004).
- **Join key** — `test_assignment_unique_on_store_and_clerk` pins the exact
  constraint name; `test_same_clerk_id_at_a_different_store_is_allowed` proves
  the UNIQUE is on the pair, which it must be, because a PIN is per-store on
  real data.
- **Store FK** — `test_assignment_store_fk_rejects_unknown_store`, matching §6's
  edge row "FK rejects; derivation fails loudly".
- **Money typing** — `test_money_columns_are_numeric_12_4` reads
  `information_schema` for precision and scale rather than trusting the DDL.

21 tests, all passing. Full adhoc sweep green on the rebased branch.

## What surprised me

That the most consequential line in this migration is invisible to the gate, and
that I only found out because I read the drift output carefully instead of
skimming for "green". Four tables appeared and the check reported four tables;
everything looked complete. The `ALTER` widening admin access was simply not in
the vocabulary the snapshot speaks.

The second surprise is smaller. I expected the role question to be a bench item
and it was not — the read-upward rule resolved it cleanly, and the thing that
had felt like a contradiction turned out to be two coherent vocabularies with
one incorrect comment sitting between them. It is the first time in this chain
that pulling a thread produced *less* ambiguity than it started with, and after
B-0022 through B-0027 I had stopped expecting that.
